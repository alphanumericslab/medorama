Connected databases

Medorama reads each one where it is and never writes it. What it keeps of one (its persons, variables, recordings' indexes, results) is in its own folder of the workspace, encrypted with its own key; removing a database destroys the key, then the folder, and takes its patients out of every cohort.

Connect a database

A database names each patient by an identifier and who issued it (an MRN and its hospital, a study ID and its study); two databases that name a patient alike are linked as one patient, exactly. Tables, exports and folders of recordings become a database with db-builder first (docs/databases.md).

Create a database…db-builder opens in a tab of its own, with its own sign-in; connect here what it builds.

Add-ons

Adapters: programs that read recording formats Medorama does not read itself (licensed formats). They are installed on the server, by an administrator: medorama addons install PROGRAM; never through this page.

Dates and times

How every page writes dates and times, for everyone, in Medorama's time zone (, set in its medorama.toml). Exports and the API keep ISO 8601 (2024-01-31 14:05), which every program reads.

Privacy Mode

What Privacy Mode withholds, for every user, on every page, in every file and in exports. It is always on for users without the PHI permission, and on by default for everyone but admins (the header's Privacy Mode switch). The server replaces what is withheld by placeholders ([Patient Name], [MRN] …) and patients by their pseudonyms. Pictures, PDFs, DICOM pixels and videos are sent as they are: their tabs, and the patient page above them, are marked with an amber triangle (▲).

Always withheld, and not a choice: patients' names, medical record numbers, dates of birth, account and encounter numbers (CSN).

Dates move back by the offset below, and each patient's by a number of days of their own on top (from 0 up to the days set here, at least 30 and at most 3650). A patient's own days are drawn once and kept, so every user and session sees the same dates for them; the time between any two of a patient's events is kept. The offset is counted on the calendar from 2000-01-01. Changing either changes the dates shown for every patient.
